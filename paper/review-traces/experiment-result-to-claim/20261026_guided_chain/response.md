## Derivative failure: location is established, surface mechanism is not

The failures are 12 probe/row combinations across five unique geometry rows: times 15.609375, 15.71484375, 15.8203125, 20.4609375 and 20.671875 s. All use geometry IDs 0/149. Compile-only metadata identifies these as `v7_R1`, a world sphere of radius 0.035 m, and `collision_0068` on body `back_arm_link`, a mesh. This is not enough to attribute the failure to another obstacle or another arm.

All 12 combinations fail `Jd` versus half-step central derivative; only one also fails full-step versus half-step central derivative (probe 3, row 195: error 0.230380 versus tolerance 0.183386). The code uses one-sided coordinate differences at h=1e-4, while the normalized directional probes use ±h and ±h/2 and fixed row/time identities. Most failing directional central differences are stable even while their coordinate-Jacobian projection is not. This localizes an inconsistent numerical linearization; it does not distinguish forward-difference truncation, mesh closest-feature changes, signed-distance behavior in penetration, or upstream propagation sensitivity. No closest-point witnesses or feature IDs were saved, and the internal FD mass matrices/full trajectories are unavailable. A proven closest-feature-switch root cause would overstate the evidence.

A suitable future repair specification keeps the same physical gates and derivative tolerance, preserves failing row/time identities, and adds diagnostic archival before potentially failing calls. It should distinguish step-size/truncation effects from distance-feature changes using symmetric coordinate derivatives, a prescribed convergence check and feature/witness information where available. Merely changing h, substituting central differences, excluding R1, deleting failed rows, changing mesh geometry, or relaxing tolerances is not a validated repair. These are design considerations for a separately authorized unit; no supplementary physical run is authorized by this review, and the sealed run must not be repaired in place.
